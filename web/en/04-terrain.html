SELF-PACED WORKSHOP

Adapt walking to slopes

Adapt walking to slopes. Explore the concepts, run the lab, and inspect its results.

Chapter 4 · Self-study and practice

Start with a testable question

Do not assume the old actor will fail. Hold the policy, command, duration, friction and initial-state distribution fixed while varying slope. Test traversal and balance, not just a single attractive frame.

Build a flat–ramp–landing scene

The surface has a 0.35 m flat approach, a 1 m horizontal ramp, and a landing. Positive angles ascend; negative angles descend. A continuous collision mesh avoids unintended steps. Visual materials do not determine contact dynamics.

坡面高度
\[h(x)=\tan(\alpha)\,\operatorname{clip}(x-0.35,0,1)\]

slope = math.tan(math.radians(cfg.slope_deg))
xs = [0., 2.35, 3.35, 6.]
zs = [0., 0., slope, slope]
vertices = [(x, y, z) for x, z in zip(xs, zs) for y in [0., 4.]]

The mesh uses offset local coordinates; the robot spawn corresponds to x = 0 in the equation. Isaac Lab TerrainGenerator and TerrainImporter reuse this geometry for visualization, physics and parallel tasks.

Test the official walking policy first

Terminal
python3 scripts/workshop.py terrain \
  --model models/walk.onnx \
  --slope 5 --num-envs 1 --seconds 20 --seed 42001 \
  --output outputs/slope-up-05

Without --headless this opens Isaac Sim. Use --num-envs 5 --headless for batched evaluation, and test 0, ±5, ±10 and ±15 degrees in separate output directories. The first 2 s are standing; then the command is 0.25 m/s. Actual speed may differ.

Success requires reaching x ≥ 1.45 m within 20 s with lateral error below 0.3 m and staying upright throughout. A fall remains a failure even if the body later slides across the finish. Reports separate reaching, falls and velocity RMSE.

Why flat-ground experience may not transfer

A slope changes foot clearance, gravity along the surface and usable friction. Insufficient clearance or propulsion can stall ascent; downhill landing and balance changes can cause falls. Survival alone misses stalled traversal.

坡面上的重力分量与简化摩擦约束
\[F_{\parallel}=mg\sin\alpha,\qquad N\approx mg\cos\alpha,\qquad |F_{\mathrm{tangent}}|\leq\mu N\]

These are quasi-static intuitions, not a maximum-slope guarantee. Newton advances dynamics, contact and actuators at every physics step. Isaac Lab 3 separates the learning task from its physics backend; this lesson retains native Newton actuators.

Continue learning across terrain types

Keep the official ONNX unchanged. Initialize a new actor from its weights and normalization, with a fresh critic and optimizer. Mix 25% flat terrain with ascending and descending terrain. Verify initial actor parity before learning.

Terminal
python3 scripts/workshop.py train-terrain \
  --model models/walk.onnx \
  --num-envs 256 --iterations 1000 --seed 43001 \
  --output outputs/terrain-training-01

Training slopes are tan(α), not degrees: 0.03–0.22 is about 1.7°–12.4°. Training uses pyramidal and inverted slopes; evaluation uses straight ramps and separate seeds.

Balance and direction both matter

After adapting balance, check direction. Small turn-rate errors can accumulate into missed targets. A separate body-z angular-velocity term avoids mixing turn accuracy with ordinary gait roll and pitch; posture and smoothness terms remain.

转向速度跟踪奖励
\[r_{\omega}=\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\omega_z-\omega_z^*}{0.15}\right)^2\right]\]
Python · 核心代码节选Full source ↗
def yaw_rate_tracking(env, std=.15):
    velocity = env.scene['robot'].data.root_link_ang_vel_b.torch
    target = env.command_manager.get_command('base_velocity')[:, 2]
    return torch.exp(-((velocity[:, 2] - target) / std).square())

cfg.rewards.yaw_precision = RewardTermCfg(
    func=yaw_rate_tracking, weight=4., params={'std': .15})

Both angular velocities use rad/s; 0.15 sets tolerance and weight4 controls relative importance. This is not a task-success metric. Resume the previous checkpoint to improve the existing motion rather than restarting from random parameters.

Terminal
python3 scripts/workshop.py train-terrain \
  --model models/walk.onnx \
  --resume outputs/terrain-training-01/model_999.pt \
  --refine-heading --num-envs 256 --iterations 1500 --seed 43002 \
  --output outputs/terrain-heading-01

python3 scripts/workshop.py train-terrain \
  --model models/walk.onnx \
  --resume outputs/terrain-heading-01/model_2498.pt \
  --refine-heading --yaw-precision \
  --num-envs 256 --iterations 2000 --seed 43003 \
  --output outputs/terrain-direction-01

The first continuation strengthens angular tracking and fixes nominal physical parameters. The second adds the separate turn-rate reward. Broad friction, mass and sensor-noise robustness is outside this controlled experiment. If you change iteration budgets, use the checkpoint filenames actually saved. Every stage keeps separate logs and exports.

The fixed terrain actor uses256 environments and4500 total iterations. Recorded training stages on DGX Spark took about8.1,9.8 and13.2 minutes, totaling31.1 minutes, excluding environment initialization and evaluation. Each iteration collects256×24=6144 transitions. Parallel worlds share a policy but provide varied experience; scaling is not necessarily linear.

Terminal
python3 scripts/workshop.py tensorboard \
  --logdir outputs/terrain-direction-01 --port 6006

Inspect tracking rewards, terrain difficulty and episode length in TensorBoard. Curves diagnose learning; the matched benchmark checks task completion. For a shorter exercise, stop after the first1000 iterations and use the fixed course terrain actor downstream.

Compare both actors with the same benchmark

Terminal
python3 scripts/workshop.py terrain \
  --model models/terrain-walk.onnx \
  --slope 5 --num-envs 5 --seconds 20 --seed 44001 --headless \
  --output outputs/terrain-evaluation-up-05

Use the fixed0.79 MB terrain actor by default. Explicitly select outputs/terrain-direction-01/exported/policy.onnx to test your own export. Chapters1 and7 retain the original walking actor.

Use matched evaluation seeds and angles for both actors. Report flat, uphill and downhill success, falls, tracking error and training time. Stalling is not success, and slope improvements do not justify losing flat-ground ability.
