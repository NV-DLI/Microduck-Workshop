Keep the course detector downstream

Inspect labels and detections

The sample has 40 images split into 24 training, 8 validation, and 8 test images. Class 0 is the ball and class 1 the goal. Each YOLO label row is class cx cy width height, with the last four values normalized by image dimensions. A horizontal center of 0.5 means halfway across the image, not 0.5 meters in the world.

Choose one clear prediction and one difficult example. Check missed objects, false detections, and misplaced boxes, then compare object size and position. High confidence cannot repair camera calibration or turn a 2D box into depth. Navigation still requires range, attitude, timestamps, and validity checks.

What must adapt when the scene changes?

Domain randomization exposes a model to meaningful variation during training. Apply it to the affected component: mat colours, markings, light and mesh affect RGB detection; friction, voltage, delay, mass and contacts affect locomotion and kicking. This system combines detection, geometry, Nav2 and action policies; it does not use a vision-language-action model such as GR00T.

This exercise fixes the goal shape, size and material. Visual variation covers two football surfaces, marble, wood, illumination, ball position and camera pose. The motivation resembles varying tube placement and colour for GR00T, but this system uses separate detector and motion policies; only the detector was fine-tuned for this scene, and GR00T is not used.

Check your understanding

Recall the slope-walking training in Chapter 4. Does adapting Microduck to different slopes count as Domain Randomization? Does this implementation also use Curriculum Learning? Identify evidence for random variation and difficulty adjustment before revealing the answer.

Show explanation

This course's training uses both terrain domain randomization and curriculum learning, but training on several slopes alone would not establish that.

Where is the randomization? The training entry point defines 5 difficulty rows and 8 terrain columns: 2 flat, 3 pyramidal and 3 inverted pyramidal. Types are assigned by proportion, not randomly drawn each episode. When generating the terrain, each tile receives difficulty d = (r + u) / 5, where the row index is r = 0,…,4 and u is sampled uniformly from [0, 1). The slope parameter's magnitude is 0.03 + d × (0.22 − 0.03). This is tan(α), not degrees; the configured range corresponds to about 1.7°–12.4°. Random variation in slope geometry at generation time qualifies as terrain domain randomization. A new slope is not drawn from the entire range at every reset.

Where is the curriculum? Training sets curriculum=True and max_init_terrain_level=1, and retains the rough-terrain task's terrain_levels_vel update. Environments start at randomly assigned level 0 or 1. At resets, levels change according to horizontal displacement from the environment origin: above 2 m advances a level; otherwise, displacement below the current translational command magnitude × episode time limit × 0.5 lowers the level. Levels are bounded below by 0; advancing beyond the highest level assigns a random level. Difficulty therefore adapts to performance, rather than following a fixed iteration schedule from flat ground to steep slopes.

How do they differ? Domain randomization concerns random variation in training environments; curriculum learning concerns how difficulty is scheduled. They can be combined. Testing fixed straight ramps at 0, ±5°, ±10° and ±15° in Chapter 4 is an evaluation protocol, not domain randomization by itself. This classification also does not establish a real-world performance benefit from randomization alone.

Check the implementation: training configuration; the course-pinned Isaac Lab terrain sampling, promotion and demotion criteria, and level assignment.

The official black-and-white ball, its diameter, the sloped-back goal and new surfaces change the visual input. On the same 128 new test images, the previous indoor detector scores mAP50 0.379, with ball AP50 only 0.020. We therefore fine-tune the detector while preserving walking, kicking and residual weights. The dataset has 48 scene seeds and 768 images; all surfaces and views sharing a seed stay in the same split.

The detector was fine-tuned for 60 epochs using 512 training, 128 validation and 128 test images. Held-out mAP50 is 0.978 and mAP50–95 is 0.934. These measure detection quality, not autonomous task success or the isolated benefit of randomization; that requires a matched-data, matched-budget ablation. Localization, paths, kicks, goal crossing and recovery require separate task tests.

Let failures determine whether to widen randomization. Test one fixed model on all four surfaces, then on unseen lighting and ball positions. Lost ball contours near white lines point to perception; correct recognition and stance followed by slip or failed recovery point to dynamics. Avoid changing every parameter at once, which obscures the cause.

Check your understanding: can lighting randomization fix localization after widening the goal but keeping the old centre spacing and map? No. Converting pixels to metres depends on the actual dimensions; correct the geometry contract first.

This fine-tuning run used 60 epochs and about 4.8 minutes of training wall time, excluding collection, export and complete simulations. Appearance and light variation target perception. Changing known goal dimensions also requires matching localization priors, maps and scoring, or a separate method that estimates unknown dimensions.

The teacher and residual in walking shots

The course kick combines a fixed base policy with a trained residual model. The residual adjusts the base behavior, so observations, action scale, actuators, and timing must match. Removing one model does not leave an equivalent skill.

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-teacher.onnx
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-residual.onnx

Find both files in the model inventory. Compare their input and output shapes and distinguish robot-state inputs from task inputs. The complete task uses the pair in Chapter 7; retraining is not required here.

Understand the complete walking-shot system

This is not a single network issuing a “score” command. A walking policy controls the body, a detector supplies visual targets, range and attitude constrain relative position, a state machine decides when to observe, walk, and kick, and the kick models execute contact and recovery.

Run the complete teaching scene

Run the command below from the repository root to start autonomous football. Use a new output directory for video, sensor records and scoring.

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py football \
  --scene indoor --style A --record-hd \
  --seed 983102 \
  --seconds 180 \
  --output outputs/football-01

Expected result: an Isaac simulation window opens and one robot searches, approaches, aligns, and attempts a kick autonomously. The program uses the repository’s fixed models and saves video, traces, and results. The 180-second budget is simulation time, not wall-clock waiting time. Initialization and rendering can take longer.

This seed is a selected teaching fixture drawn from successful examples. It supports comparison on the same scene and is not a new independent success-rate sample. Do not manually move the robot or ball during execution. To stop, press Ctrl+C in the terminal and retain the logs.

The indoor fixture starts the robot at (−0.55, 0.04) m and ball at (0.02, 0) m, with the goal plane at x = 0.68 m. Robot and ball coordinates initialize the scene; goal position and dimensions are known map priors. Runtime robot and ball positions still come from sensor estimates. Select patterns with --style B, C or D, and change starts with --root-y or --ball-xy. Record altered conditions as separate tests.

Gliding shot: observe, make contact and stop early

The gliding task uses the same 10 cm ball, goal, camera and detector. It loads the official roller actor from initialization to the end. Its wheels are passive, with propulsion and braking produced by leg motion. Wheel–ball contact transfers momentum without switching to walking or executing a leg kick. The fixed start is about 30 cm behind the ball: this tests short empty-field approach and shooting, not navigation from arbitrary field positions.

The controller detects the goal and ball and estimates approach direction from pixels, camera geometry and a ToF ground plane. It slows, aligns and gradually returns head control to the roller actor. After settling, it propels briefly and adjusts braking using estimated velocity while remaining on its wheels. Waiting for perception also requires coasting control: releasing propulsion is not a stop.

This visual-feedback branch does not integrate Nav2 or walking sole odometry. Braking velocity is integrated from ideal trunk-COM specific force, gyro and gravity-direction measurements, starting at zero velocity. The controller does not read true robot velocity or ball coordinates. IMU mounting offsets, bias and vibration are not modeled, so this is not a hardware stopping solution. The command below uses the evaluation configuration: no window or additional live observer recording, while still rendering RGB/ToF and saving sensors, actions and physics. Watch the full demonstration above and use a new output directory per run. A fixed seed does not guarantee success.

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py football \
  --locomotion rollers --seed 42 --style A \
  --headless --no-record-hd \
  --seconds 40 \
  --output outputs/football-rollers-01

Inspect the result with the command below. Check your understanding

Why can the task fail even when detection mAP is high and the walking policy stays upright?

Show explanation

Success depends on interfaces between modules. Targets can leave view, coordinates or observation age can become unsuitable, and position or heading may fail the kick conditions. Module metrics do not replace end-to-end evaluation.

Locate a problem from a failed run

First check the result file: result.json for walking or independent-result.json for rollers. If not, inspect the terminal error and installation or runtime conditions: that is an execution failure. If results exist, distinguish failure to find the ball, failure to reach a kicking stance, contact without a goal, and failure to recover after scoring. A normal process exit does not mean the robot completed the task.

For walking, course_evaluation.passed combines physical scoring and applicable checks. scorer retains contact, goal crossing, and fall information. In synchronized views, inspect the stance before first contact and posture after kicking. A short clip of the ball crossing the line is insufficient for a full-task judgment. For rollers, autonomous_task_passed marks complete success; wheel contact, whole-ball crossing and continuous settling are reported separately.
