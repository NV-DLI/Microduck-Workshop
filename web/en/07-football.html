Complete the autonomous task

Implement detection, localization, stance planning, approach and skill handoff, then run the complete task in Isaac Sim.

Chapter 7 · About 40 minutes · GPU

Run the fixed detector first

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py detect \
  --model models/indoor-detector/detector.pt \
  --source datasets/indoor-sample/images \
  --output outputs/detection-01

Expected result: images with ball and goal detections. Inspect small targets, image boundaries, and occlusion. Confidence is a model score, not a guarantee that a detection is correct.

This detector is adapted to the steel-mesh goal and marked mat. Eight illustrations from the held-out image split cover four patterns; the original detector and exercise dataset remain available. Bounding boxes still require post-base extraction and known-dimension localization before planning.

Practice training a detector

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py train-detector \
  --data datasets/sample/data.yaml \
  --epochs 5 \
  --batch 8 \
  --seed 303 \
  --output outputs/student-detector-01

The small dataset and five epochs introduce data, labels, training, and validation; this is not a full training recipe. Inspect loss curves and validation images. To improve quality, diversify scenes and keep training, validation, and test scenes separate.

Keep the course detector downstream

Chapters 6 and 7 use models/detector.pt by default. The practice model does not replace it. Detection mAP measures detection quality, not shooting success.

Inspect labels and detections

The sample has 40 images split into 24 training, 8 validation, and 8 test images. Class 0 is the ball and class 1 the goal. Each YOLO label row is class cx cy width height, with the last four values normalized by image dimensions. A horizontal center of 0.5 means halfway across the image, not 0.5 meters in the world.

Choose one clear prediction and one difficult example. Check missed objects, false detections, and misplaced boxes, then compare object size and position. High confidence cannot repair camera calibration or turn a 2D box into depth. Navigation still requires range, attitude, timestamps, and validity checks.

Check your understanding

Can a ball label centered at (0.6, 0.4) be used directly as a navigation target at (0.6 m, 0.4 m)?

Show explanation

No. These are normalized image coordinates. A camera model and range or ground constraint are needed to obtain robot-relative position.

What must adapt when the scene changes?

Domain randomization exposes a model to meaningful variation during training. Apply it to the affected component: mat colours, markings, light and mesh affect RGB detection; friction, voltage, delay, mass and contacts affect locomotion and kicking. This system combines detection, geometry, Nav2 and action policies; it does not use a vision-language-action model such as GR00T.

VariationMain effectResponse
Mat A–D, light, camera poseBoxes and contoursGenerate varied RGB data; split by scene
Goal size/location and ball diameterMetric scale, map and scoringUpdate geometry contracts, not only textures
Friction, voltage, delay and massGait, impact and recoveryMeasure ranges; test whether policy adaptation is needed
Unknown obstacles, edges or deformationTraversability and contactExtend sensing/physics and then test planning/control

The steel-mesh goal and marked mat changed detection: development images showed missed goals and field-line false positives with the original detector. Perception was therefore fine-tuned while the walking, kicking and residual weights were preserved. The dataset contains four mat patterns with lighting, position and camera variations: 48 scene seeds and 768 images. All patterns and views from one scene belong to one split to avoid leakage.

The detector was fine-tuned for 60 epochs using 512 training, 128 validation and 128 test images. Held-out mAP50 is 0.945 and mAP50–95 is 0.920. These measure detection quality, not autonomous task success or the isolated benefit of randomization; that requires a matched-data, matched-budget ablation. Localization, paths, kicks, goal crossing and recovery require separate task tests.

Let failures determine whether to widen randomization. Test one fixed model on all four mats, then on unseen lighting and ball positions. Lost ball contours near white lines point to perception; correct recognition and stance followed by slip or failed recovery point to dynamics. Avoid changing every parameter at once, which obscures the cause.

Check your understanding: can lighting randomization fix localization after widening the goal but keeping the old centre spacing and map? No. Converting pixels to metres depends on the actual dimensions; correct the geometry contract first.

This fine-tuning run used 60 epochs and about 4.3 minutes of training wall time, excluding collection, export and complete simulations. Appearance and light variation target perception. Changing known goal dimensions also requires matching localization priors, maps and scoring, or a separate method that estimates unknown dimensions.

Understand a teacher and a residual

The course kick combines a fixed base policy with a trained residual model. The residual adjusts the base behavior, so observations, action scale, actuators, and timing must match. Removing one model does not leave an equivalent skill.

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-teacher.onnx
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-residual.onnx

Find both files in the model inventory. Compare their input and output shapes and distinguish robot-state inputs from task inputs. The complete task uses the pair in Chapter 7; retraining is not required here.

Understand the complete system

This is not a single network issuing a “score” command. A walking policy controls the body, a detector supplies visual targets, range and attitude constrain relative position, a state machine decides when to observe, walk, and kick, and the kick models execute contact and recovery.

Run the complete teaching scene

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py football \
  --scene indoor --style A --record-hd \
  --seed 983102 \
  --seconds 180 \
  --output outputs/football-01

Expected result: an Isaac simulation window opens and one robot searches, approaches, aligns, and attempts a kick autonomously. The program uses the repository’s fixed models and saves video, traces, and results. The 180-second budget is simulation time, not wall-clock waiting time. Initialization and rendering can take longer.

This seed is a selected teaching fixture drawn from successful examples. It supports comparison on the same scene and is not a new independent success-rate sample. Do not manually move the robot or ball during execution. To stop, press Ctrl+C in the terminal and retain the logs.

The indoor fixture starts the robot at (−0.55, 0.04) m and ball at (0.02, 0) m, with the goal plane at x = 0.68 m. Robot and ball coordinates initialize the scene; goal position and dimensions are known map priors. Runtime robot and ball positions still come from sensor estimates. Select patterns with --style B, C or D, and change starts with --root-y or --ball-xy. Record altered conditions as separate tests.

Judge complete success

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py report --run outputs/football-01
  • A real foot–ball contact occurred.
  • The entire ball crossed the goal plane inside the opening.
  • The robot did not fall or exceed the specified mechanical bounds.
  • There was no premature ball contact, and the robot recovered after kicking.
Interpreting the reference result

The original simple field and narrower goal completed 9/10 independent tasks. That historical result does not transfer to the marked mat, steel-mesh goal and full-body contacts. The current A-pattern demonstration completed contact, whole-ball crossing and recovery in 17.485 task seconds, passing all 11 applicable checks. It is a development example, not an independent success-rate estimate. New pattern tests must remain separate.

Four predeclared new seeds at the same standard start, one per mat, completed 1/4 full tasks. Passing applicable record audits is distinct from task success. Pattern and seed vary together, so this does not isolate a pattern effect or estimate broad reliability. See the new-scene trial records.

All three failures occurred before the kick: B estimated the ball outside the kick range, then could not satisfy the current-body guard during retreat recovery; its ball observation had also expired by the stop; C cycled through reacquisition and alignment until the 180-second limit; D stopped its fine approach because a fresh goal observation was unavailable. None fell. A new scene therefore needs more than refreshed media or a strong detection score. Inspect target-point localization, observation validity and planning handoff before choosing additional training data; these trials do not establish a failure of the walking or kicking policies.

Design a small evaluation

Write down seeds and success criteria before running. Record goals, falls, contact, and recovery for every episode. Do not replace a failure with a retry. A model or parameter change defines a separate experiment.

Check your understanding

Why can the task fail even when detection mAP is high and the walking policy stays upright?

Show explanation

Success depends on interfaces between modules. Targets can leave view, coordinates or observation age can become unsuitable, and position or heading may fail the kick conditions. Module metrics do not replace end-to-end evaluation.

Locate a problem from a failed run

First check whether result.json was produced. If not, inspect the terminal error and installation or runtime conditions: that is an execution failure. If results exist, distinguish failure to find the ball, failure to reach a kicking stance, contact without a goal, and failure to recover after scoring. A normal process exit does not mean the robot completed the task.

course_evaluation.passed combines physical scoring and applicable checks. scorer retains contact, goal crossing, and fall information. In synchronized views, inspect the stance before first contact and posture after kicking. A short clip of the ball crossing the line is insufficient for a full-task judgment.

The original simple-field combination completed 9/10 independent tasks. The new mat and steel goal change appearance, geometry and contacts, requiring separate evaluation. Default seed 983102 demonstrates the new scene; repetitions are not independent samples.
