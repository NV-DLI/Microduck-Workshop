SELF-PACED WORKSHOP

Teach GR00T from demonstrations

Understand GR00T 1.7 through football: observations become actions, demonstrations become skills, and failures guide corrective data.

Chapter 7 · Estimated learning time: 35 minutes

From writing steps to providing goals and examples

Give Microduck the instruction “send the ball into the goal” alongside demonstrations of observations and actions. A vision-language-action model (VLA) connects images, language and robot state to predict what to do next. Language expresses the goal; demonstrations show how this robot can achieve it.

GR00T N1.7 is a foundation model that can be post-trained for a robot and task. General visual knowledge helps interpret situations, but an instruction alone does not teach Microduck balance, skating brakes or kick timing. Compatible action interfaces and task demonstrations remain necessary.

How GR00T 1.7 turns observations into actions

Cosmos-Reason2 is the visual-language backbone used by GR00T 1.7. Chapter 9 discusses Cosmos 3 world models and data generation. These related names refer to different roles in the learning workflow.

Pretraining, fine-tuning and inference

StageInputs and changesFootball interpretation

Post-training / fine-tuningRobot/task data update selected parametersLearn Microduck actions and football behavior
InferenceCurrent observations enter fixed weights; actions come outRepeated observation and execution

Fine-tuning is a common form of post-training. Freezing selected parameters reduces training cost, but frozen components still perform computation. A training step is an optimizer update, not a robot step or one simulation frame.

Predict a chunk; choose how much to execute

An action chunk predicts H future actions. The executor may apply only the first K before predicting again from a new observation, with K ≤ H. Long execution risks stale actions after the ball moves; very short execution puts more demand on inference and communication.

Nominal command duration
\[T_{\mathrm{execute}} = K / f_{\mathrm{action}}\]

For timing illustration only, five actions at 50 Hz represent 0.1 seconds of command time, excluding inference and network delay. This is not a recommended GR00T training setting. H, K and action rate must fit the data, model latency and closed-loop behavior.

Define what an action means. Joint targets require learning more balance and contact detail; velocity, gaze and skill requests can reuse low-level policies. Either is a research option, but labels, processors and execution must agree. The walking actor’s 14 normalized outputs are neither 14 angles in radians nor velocity commands.

Teach one simple field first

Good demonstrations require more than eventually scoring. Identical starts leave small deviations uncovered, while repeated hesitation may teach unnecessary movements. First make the traditional method accurate and smooth over a reasonable range of starts on one field, then use it as a demonstration source.

Recovering from drift: HG-DAgger and automated experts

After the first deviation, the policy may encounter observations absent from demonstrations. If skating drifts to one side, imitating a straight approach may worsen the error. Successful trajectories alone often omit recovery from off-course states.

Automated takeover is worth studying for Microduck because it can reuse validated controls. High accuracy from standard starts is insufficient: the expert must handle moved balls, heading errors, existing motion and briefly lost targets. Improve recovery or stop/reset where it cannot recover; do not train on failed corrections.

A biped cannot simply pause joint commands like a fixed-base arm. Clear stale action queues during takeover, maintain balance or controlled braking, and assign head/body ownership explicitly. If a simulation expert uses exact positions, label that privileged information and exclude it from the future real-robot policy inputs.

Connect Microduck to a capable computer

Microduck’s onboard computer is not suitable for running a large VLA such as GR00T. A real robot can communicate with a computer equipped with a suitable GPU: the robot streams images and state, the computer runs inference and returns action requests. Choose hardware based on the model, precision and measured latency.

A smarter model does not remove control engineering. It predicts actions, while local execution maintains balance, manages freshness and handles interrupted communication. The next chapter lets an agent organize reliable action skills into longer tasks.
