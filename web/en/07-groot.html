5.1 Vision-language-action models and GR00T#

Section 4.3 used hand-designed stages to decide when to approach and kick. Now let a model learn those decisions from instructions and demonstrations.

Section 5.1 · Estimated learning time: 40 minutes

From instructions to actions#

A vision-language-action model (VLA) reads images, a language instruction and robot state, then predicts a sequence of actions. Language specifies the goal; demonstrations teach how to achieve it. Actions may be joint targets or requests to existing skills.

How GR00T generates actions#

GR00T N1.7 has about 3 billion parameters. Its Cosmos-Reason2-2B vision-language model backbone extracts image and instruction features. A flow-matching action head combines these with robot state to generate actions. System 2 and System 1 name these components; they do not imply asynchronous control.

Cosmos-Reason2 is the internal backbone. Cosmos 3 in Section 5.3 serves a different purpose: extending data.

Why predict a sequence#

An action chunk supports continuity and reduces inference calls. Usually only a prefix executes before observing again: longer execution reduces calls but delays corrections. Section 5.2 distinguishes prediction length from execution length.

Pretraining, post-training and inference#

Stage

Purpose

Pretraining

Learn general representations from images, text, video, simulation and real robot data

Post-training

Update selected parameters using Microduck observations and demonstrated actions

Inference

Keep weights fixed and predict from new observations

Fine-tuning is one form of post-training. Frozen modules still compute but their parameters do not update. A training step is an optimizer update, not a robot step. Our post-training demonstrations come from simulation; Section 5.3 discusses synthetic data for visual variation.

Imitation and reinforcement learning#

Imitation learning (IL) trains a policy from a teacher's observation-action pairs. Success labels or video without action labels are insufficient for this action interface.

Reward design and exploration cost

They can work together: GR00T imitates task requests and calls skills trained with RL. A single policy could also learn by imitation before RL refinement; our experiment does not update GR00T using task rewards.

How to assess learning#

Low training loss measures fit to demonstrations. Test complete tasks from starts excluded from training and tuning, checking goals, falls and recovery. New lighting, paraphrased instructions and new tasks require separate tests. One successful video does not establish reliability or generalization.

Our model trains on one pitch and one instruction. Compare it with onboard control using the same starts, skills and success criteria, considering success, latency, demonstration volume and training cost. A larger model is not automatically a better choice.
