SELF-PACED WORKSHOP

From actions to complete skills

Break a kick into preparation, contact, withdrawal, and recovery. Understand why skills need entry and exit conditions.

Chapter 4 · About 25 minutes

Define a skill’s boundaries

Walking continuously accepts velocity commands. A kick is a short skill with a start and an end. Integrating it into an autonomous task requires entry conditions, a defined exit state, and a way to stop when conditions are unsafe.

PhaseWhat to check
PrepareBall in the kick region, acceptable heading, and fresh observations
ContactReal foot contact moves the ball; ball state is not overwritten
WithdrawEnd the kick and return to support
RecoverRemain stable without falling or exceeding joint limits

Understand a teacher and a residual

The course kick combines a fixed base policy with a trained residual model. The residual adjusts the base behavior, so observations, action scale, actuators, and timing must match. Removing one model does not leave an equivalent skill.

Terminal · Bash
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-teacher.onnx
python3 scripts/workshop.py inspect-model --model models/kick-residual.onnx

Find both files in the model inventory. Compare their input and output shapes and distinguish robot-state inputs from task inputs. The complete task uses the pair in Chapter 7; retraining is not required here.

Why ONNX files are not interchangeable

ONNX is a model representation, not a universal robot-skill protocol. The walking model takes 61 inputs, while the kicking residual takes 62. Both output 14 numbers, yet they are not interchangeable. Input ordering, additional state, action scaling, teacher blending, and termination conditions are all part of the runtime contract.

After inspecting the models, record their input and output shapes. Explain why successfully loading a file only proves that its format is readable, not that the robot will perform a skill. A valid file can still receive the wrong units or joint order. The fixed task entry point uses weights together with their required conventions.

Watch the kick in three phases: before it, inspect ball and support-foot positions; during it, identify whether contact comes from the foot or another body part; afterwards, inspect recovery of support. Pause the GIF in each phase and describe the entry and completion conditions. This is more informative than asking only whether the ball moved.

Check your understanding

The ball enters the goal, but the robot falls afterward. Is the full skill successful?

Show explanation

Not under this course’s criteria. Success includes physical contact, a goal, and recovery. Checking ball position alone misses stability and motion limits.

Optional observation: balancing on a ball

Propose success criteria for balancing: duration, allowable body tilt, and whether ball motion is permitted. Finishing a training process is a program state; learning the skill requires a separate evaluation.

Describe a skill by its contract

Describe a skill using entry conditions, observations, actions, and exit conditions. A kick requires a useful right-foot ball position, goal alignment, stable support, and fresh target information. Execution combines the kicking teacher with a residual correction. It must then return to a stable standing or walking state; touching the ball alone does not finish the task.

A residual adds a constrained correction to an existing action. This reduces what must be learned from scratch but inherits the teacher’s operating range. Starting a local kick far from the ball does not make it search or approach. Navigation creates a valid handoff state; the skill performs its local action.

Check your understanding

The robot is beside the ball, but its remembered position has expired. Should the kick start immediately?

Show explanation

No. Observe again and confirm the entry conditions. Proximity alone does not guarantee valid inputs.
