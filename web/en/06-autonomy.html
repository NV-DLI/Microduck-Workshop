Not every stage needs a learned model. Detection and motion use networks; geometry, path planning and task transitions can use explicit algorithms. Isaac Sim connects the scene and sensors; Isaac Lab trains and evaluates motion skills.

Three complementary sources

SourceInformationLimitation
RGBTarget location in the imageDoes not directly provide metric distance
ToFRanges across 64 forward sectorsInvalid sectors cannot be treated as free space

The RGB detector finds image regions resembling a ball. ToF measures ranges across an 8 × 8 forward-facing grid; it is not a 360-degree lidar. A body IMU estimates attitude so image rays and ranges can be interpreted in the right frame. No single sensor supplies the complete world state.

The simulation uses GC2093 camera and VL53L8CX ToF profiles. Images are processed at 960 × 540 and 15 Hz. Lens field of view and noise settings are simulation assumptions; physical devices require calibration.

From a pixel to a spatial ray

Let a detection center be pixel (u, v), with camera focal lengths (fx, fy) and principal point (cx, cy). In a common camera convention, the viewing ray is proportional to ((u−cx)/fx, (v−cy)/fy, 1). This gives direction but cannot distinguish a small nearby object from a larger distant one. Range or a known geometric constraint is still needed.

Camera coordinates commonly use x right, y down, z forward. Here the body convention is x forward, y left, z up. Transform using the camera’s rotation and translation relative to the body rather than mixing same-named axes. Head motion changes this transform, so localization requires joint state at the appropriate time.

An 8 × 8 ToF grid does not provide 64 independent object labels. One cell may cover both floor and ball, and close reflections may be invalid. Check measurement status and range before associating a return with an image target. Treating an invalid reading as an obstacle-free direction gives navigation a false premise.

A past observation can become stale

After moving or tilting its head, the robot may lose the ball from view. Odometry can update a remembered relative position briefly, but error accumulates. Stale observations, invalid ranges, or lost ground constraints require stopping and reobserving. Missing data is not free space.

The ball center is not the destination

The task is to strike the ball toward the goal. Sending the body to the ball center risks premature contact or an unusable approach. Work backward from the kick: face the goal, leave space behind the ball and offset the body to align the kicking foot.

Standing behind the ball is a human-designed task prior. The designer encodes the shooting direction, robot geometry and skill operating range. The walking network does not infer football rules. Detection learns appearance and motion policies learn actions; geometry computes the stance and Nav2 plans toward that supplied target.

Distinguish relative offsets from map coordinates. A stage may use fixed stand-off and lateral offsets, while its target coordinates change with the ball position or shooting direction. The indoor task uses a visually estimated ball position plus explicit map priors for the known goal and field bounds; it does not discover an entire unknown pitch.

Compute the target position and heading

Express ball B and goal center G in the same planar frame, in meters. Unit vector u points from ball to goal; n points left when facing that direction. Heading is measured counterclockwise from the map +x axis.

Shooting direction and left normal
\[\mathbf u=\frac{\mathbf G-\mathbf B}{\|\mathbf G-\mathbf B\|},\qquad \mathbf n=\begin{bmatrix}-u_y\\u_x\end{bmatrix}\]
Target stance and heading
\[\mathbf P^*=\mathbf B-d\mathbf u+\ell\mathbf n,\qquad \theta^*=\operatorname{atan2}(u_y,u_x)\]

Here d is the stand-off and ℓ is the body’s leftward offset. At the target heading, the ball should lie at about (d, −ℓ) in the body frame. Thus B = P* + d u − ℓ n; rearranging gives the target formula. Positive ℓ places the body left of the shooting axis and the ball to its right, aligning a right-foot kick. Reject coincident B and G because the direction is undefined.

For B = (0.02, 0), G = (0.68, 0), d = 0.22 and ℓ = 0.035, u = (1, 0), P* = (−0.20, 0.035) m and θ* = 0°. The body is 22 cm behind the ball and 3.5 cm left of its axis. Moving the ball changes both the direction and target; these map coordinates are not a permanent kick location.

Python · geometry excerptFull source ↗
delta = [a.goal[i] - a.ball[i] for i in range(2)]
length = math.hypot(*delta)
direction = [x / length for x in delta]
normal = [-direction[1], direction[0]]
target = [
    a.ball[i] - a.distance * direction[i] + a.lateral * normal[i]
    for i in range(2)
]

This is the core of Chapter 7’s plan geometry exercise. The full entry also checks finite values, positive stand-off and distinct points. It outputs coordinates and a diagram, without requesting a navigation path. The next question is whether known space admits a route to this target.

How Nav2 turns a target into a path

Nav2 is an open-source ROS 2 navigation framework. This task uses its Navfn planner: a start pose, target pose and costmap produce a sequence of path points. The task rules above supply the target; Nav2 does not understand right-foot kicking. See the Nav2 source project.

An occupancy grid distinguishes known free space, obstacles and unknown cells. A costmap adds traversal costs, with higher costs near obstacles to encourage clearance. The robot has a footprint, so checks must consider an envelope around the path, not a zero-width line.

The configuration explicitly enables A*. Its intuition is to compare candidates using the cost already incurred plus an estimate of the remaining cost. The conceptual formula below explains goal-directed search; it is not a line-by-line description of Navfn’s potential updates and path extraction.

Conceptual A* score
\[f(n)=g(n)+h(n)\]

Here g(n) is the accumulated cost to candidate cell n, and h(n) estimates its remaining cost. These scalar search symbols are separate from goal G and the normal vector in the geometry section. The search method is selected by configuration, not learned by the walking network.

YAML · Navfn configuration excerptFull source ↗
planner_plugins: [GridBased]
GridBased:
  plugin: nav2_navfn_planner::NavfnPlanner
  use_astar: true
  tolerance: 0.025
  allow_unknown: false
  use_final_approach_orientation: false

Unknown-space planning is disabled. tolerance: 0.025 is the path-end tolerance in meters, not a foot-to-ball tolerance. The 4 × 3 m navigation grid at 2.5 cm resolution is distinct from the physical 1 × 1.6 m mat. The 0.10 m circular footprint is a navigation approximation, not a substitute for bipedal motion and contact validation. See the Navfn parameter reference.

The full task submits an immutable snapshot of timestamped observations, known field bounds, robot pose and target to a ROS 2 Jazzy Nav2 service in a separate CPU container. The bridge publishes the matching map and transforms, then calls ComputePathToPose. Returned results must match the request, coordinate frame and map snapshot. ToF observations and known empty-field priors do not establish unknown-scene mapping or general obstacle avoidance.

How does the robot execute the approach?

A conventional Nav2 stack can use a controller server to follow a path and publish velocities. This task does not enable that full execution chain. Its bridge obtains Navfn paths and traversal checks; a custom controller combines visual ball bearing, stance error and posture to generate bounded turning, forward, alignment or stop phases. The video does not show a stock Nav2 controller following every waypoint.

From body commands to joint actions
\[\mathbf c_t=(v_x^{\rm cmd},v_y^{\rm cmd},\omega_z^{\rm cmd}),\qquad \mathbf a_t=\pi_{\rm walk}(\mathbf o_t,\mathbf c_t)\]

Body commands express desired motion. The walking policy combines them with proprioception to produce 14 joint actions at 50 Hz; Newton advances physics at 200 Hz. Commands are not measured achieved velocities: gait, contact and delay introduce error, requiring feedback. Use the executor’s conventions for turning signs and velocity scaling.

How do Isaac ROS and Nav2 relate?

Nav2 is an independent open-source navigation framework, not an NVIDIA proprietary navigator. Isaac ROS provides NVIDIA-accelerated ROS components that can supply perception, localization and environmental representations to Nav2. For example, Visual SLAM can provide poses, and nvblox can reconstruct an environment from depth and poses for use through costmap interfaces. Call this Isaac ROS integration with Nav2, rather than treating them as one product.

This task uses Isaac Sim with ROS 2 Nav2; it does not currently use Isaac ROS packages. Isaac Sim renders RTX sensor data, Isaac Lab organizes learning tasks and policy interfaces, and Newton advances physics. CPU-hosted Nav2/Navfn plans while a custom controller handles stages and commands. Using Isaac Sim does not imply Isaac ROS integration. Inspect the package lock and service source.

For physical deployment or more complex fields, first identify whether localization, mapping or control needs improvement, then select components and validate interfaces, delay and task outcomes. The official Nav2 vision-navigation tutorial shows integration with NVIDIA components; its sensor and compute setup is not automatically available on Microduck.

  3. 3Fine alignment
  4. 4Check kick conditions

At a distance, reducing range is the main priority. Nearby, motion must become more precise while preserving visibility, heading, and foot placement. Being close enough is not sufficient if heading is wrong.

A planner supplies a route or direction; the walking policy executes it. A valid planned path does not prove the robot has traversed it. The system must handle tracking errors, contact, and sensor updates.

Work through a stance target

Subtract the ball position from the goal center and normalize the result to obtain the shooting direction. The stance lies behind the ball: move back by distance along that direction, then sideways by lateral to allow space for the kicking leg. This exercise defines its own offset convention; it does not replace calibrated entry values in the physical skill.

Open plan.svg to inspect the stance, ball, and goal. plan.json gives the target position and heading. Change distance from 0.18 to 0.25 with a new output directory; predict the direction of movement and check it. This command computes geometry from known points. It runs no physics and does not prove reachability.

The full task also handles yaw drift, delayed gait response, and targets leaving view at close range. Approach therefore updates observations and checks stopping conditions repeatedly. If valid range or floor support is lost, stop or retreat within constraints and observe again. Unknown space cannot be declared free space.

When should the robot look again?

A first observation supplies an initial target, but motion changes relative geometry and estimation error accumulates. The current controller verifies position before kicking. Retreat is a recovery option, not a mandatory phase.
