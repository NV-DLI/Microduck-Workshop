3.5 (Extention) community skills: reward design#

The previous section used turning-rate tracking for slope walking. Two community examples now show how to reward shifting weight and keeping feet on a ball. Focus on turning a movement requirement into a computable formula.

Section 3.5 · Reading and analysis · Estimated learning time: 20 minutes

Two reward-design examples#

These formulas and excerpts come from fixed versions of community code and are for reading; the course commands do not load these models. The authors' simulation videos illustrate the tasks.

One-leg standing: move the center-of-mass target to the support foot#

Before lifting one foot, shift weight onto the other. Move the target from the midpoint between the feet toward the support foot, then reward the actual center of mass for approaching it. The target moves as the blend progress \(\htmlData{sym=alpha_f}{\alpha}\) rises from 0 to 1; lowering the foot reverses this transition.

The centre-of-mass target moves with the blend (weight 3)
\[\htmlData{sym=p_target}{p^{*}_{xy}}=(1-\htmlData{sym=alpha_f}{\alpha})\,\htmlData{sym=p_mid}{p_{\mathrm{mid}}}+\htmlData{sym=alpha_f}{\alpha}\,\htmlData{sym=p_stance}{p_{\mathrm{stance}}},\qquad \htmlData{sym=r_com}{r_{\mathrm{com}}}=\htmlData{sym=exp}{\exp}\!\left(-\frac{\|\htmlData{sym=c_com}{c_{xy}}-\htmlData{sym=p_target}{p^{*}_{xy}}\|^2}{\htmlData{sym=sigma_c}{\sigma_c}^2}\right)\]
Variable meanings
\(p^{*}_{xy}\)
Target horizontal position of the centre of mass (m): moves from the midpoint of the feet to above the stance foot.
\(\alpha\)
Pose blend progress: 0 for two-foot standing, 1 for one-leg standing.
\(p_{\mathrm{mid}}\)
Midpoint between the two feet (horizontal position, m).
\(p_{\mathrm{stance}}\)
Horizontal position of the stance foot (m).
\(r_{\mathrm{com}}\)
Centre-of-mass target term (weight 3).
\(\exp\)
Exponential: turns a squared error into a smooth reward between 0 and 1.
\(c_{xy}\)
Horizontal position of the whole-robot centre of mass (m), computed by the simulator over all parts.
\(\sigma_c\)
Center-of-mass error scale in meters; smaller values give stricter rewards.

Smaller error gives \(\htmlData{sym=r_com}{r_{\mathrm{com}}}\) closer to 1. \(\htmlData{sym=sigma_c}{\sigma_c}\) controls the tolerance. The reward uses the whole-body center of mass, including the head, torso and limbs.

Python · reward excerptFull source ↗

1Move the target, then calculate error

stance selects the support foot and mid is the midpoint between the feet; alpha blends them. The final expression turns horizontal center-of-mass error into an exponential reward.

1
stance = torch.where((side > 0).unsqueeze(1), right, left)
mid = 0.5 * (left + right)
a = alpha.unsqueeze(1)
target_xy = (1.0 - a) * mid[:, :2] + a * stance[:, :2]
com_xy = _robot_com_xy(env, asset)
dist2 = ((com_xy - target_xy) ** 2).sum(dim=-1)
return torch.exp(-dist2 / (std ** 2))

Other terms constrain support contact, foot clearance and posture. This selected term illustrates moving the reward target with the action's progress, instead of always rewarding one stance. See the full configuration for the remaining terms.

Basketball balance: position the body and feet relative to the ball#

Once on the ball, the robot needs to stay near its top with both feet touching the surface. These two terms constrain horizontal body position and foot-to-center distance. Ball and foot positions come from simulation state for reward calculation.

Standing on the ball: over the centre, feet on the ball (weights 2 and 1)
\[\htmlData{sym=r_center}{r_{\mathrm{center}}}=\htmlData{sym=exp}{\exp}\!\left(-\frac{\|\htmlData{sym=p_ball}{p_{\mathrm{ball}}}-\htmlData{sym=p_root}{p_{\mathrm{root}}}\|_{xy}^2}{0.04^2}\right),\qquad \htmlData{sym=r_feet}{r_{\mathrm{feet}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathrm{L,R}}\htmlData{sym=exp}{\exp}\!\left[-\left(\frac{\|\htmlData{sym=p_foot}{p_{\mathrm{foot}}}-\htmlData{sym=p_ball}{p_{\mathrm{ball}}}\|-(\htmlData{sym=R_ball}{R}+0.012)}{0.02}\right)^2\right]\]
Variable meanings
\(r_{\mathrm{center}}\)
Body-over-ball-centre term (weight 2).
\(\exp\)
Exponential: turns a squared error into a smooth reward between 0 and 1.
\(p_{\mathrm{ball}}\)
Position of the basketball centre (m), read directly from the simulation state; the deployed policy cannot see it.
\(p_{\mathrm{root}}\)
Position of the robot root (body) (m).
\(r_{\mathrm{feet}}\)
Feet-resting-on-the-ball term (weight 1), averaged over both feet.
\(p_{\mathrm{foot}}\)
Position of one foot's sole reference point (m).
\(R\)
Ball radius: 0.12 m.

The first term rewards keeping the body above the ball center. The second rewards each foot for staying at “ball radius + 12 mm sole gap” from the center, then averages the two feet. Smaller deviations earn larger rewards.

Python · reward excerptFull source ↗

1Reward feet for touching the ball

d is each foot’s distance error from the intended ball surface; std is 0.02 m. Calculate the reward per foot, then average.

1
robot = env.scene[asset_cfg.name]
feet = robot.data.site_pos_w[:, asset_cfg.site_ids, :]
d = torch.linalg.norm(feet - _bb_ball_pos(env)[:, None, :], dim=-1) - (ball_radius + sole_gap)
return torch.exp(-(d * d) / (std * std)).mean(dim=-1)

This illustrates defining a target through the geometry of the task object. Access to the true ball position for training rewards does not imply that the deployed policy can observe it directly. See the full configuration for the other terms.

From movement skills to a complete task#

The examples express task requirements through a moving target and geometric relationships with an object. Shooting also requires locating the ball, approaching it and deciding when to kick. Part 4 connects perception to movement skills, starting with RGB detection and ToF ranging in Section 4.1.
