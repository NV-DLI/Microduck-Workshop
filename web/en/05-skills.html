Explore community movement skills

Explore jumping, ball balancing and skill handoffs using the same observation–action–reward framework.

Chapter 5 · Estimated learning time: 20 minutes

What else can the same robot learn?

Community projects train jumping and ball balancing using different tasks, rewards and initial states. These demonstrations come from their original simulation environments and do not establish equivalent Newton or hardware performance.

SkillObservations and actionsRewards and completion
JumpingBody state and a jump command → joint actionsHeight, landing posture and recovery
Basketball balanceRobot and ball state → joint actionsBalance, relative position and survival
WalkingBody state and velocity command → joint actionsSpeed tracking, stability, energy and smoothness

Isaac Sim supplies props and contact geometry; Isaac Lab organizes observations, rewards and reset distributions. Equal observation dimensions do not guarantee compatibility: the jump policy repurposes a command field as a launch trigger.

Define a skill’s boundaries

Walking continuously accepts velocity commands. A kick is a short skill with a start and an end. Integrating it into an autonomous task requires entry conditions, a defined exit state, and a way to stop when conditions are unsafe.

Why ONNX files are not interchangeable

ONNX is a model representation, not a universal robot-skill protocol. The walking model takes 61 inputs, while the kicking residual takes 62. Both output 14 numbers, yet they are not interchangeable. Input ordering, additional state, action scaling, teacher blending, and termination conditions are all part of the runtime contract.

Use the model inspection commands in Chapter 7, then record input and output shapes. A readable model file does not guarantee a correct action: units and joint ordering must also match. The fixed task entry point pairs weights with their runtime contract.

Watch the kick in three phases: before it, inspect ball and support-foot positions; during it, identify whether contact comes from the foot or another body part; afterwards, inspect recovery of support. Pause the video in each phase and describe the entry and completion conditions. This is more informative than asking only whether the ball moved.

Describe a skill by its contract

Describe a skill using entry conditions, observations, actions, and exit conditions. A kick requires a useful right-foot ball position, goal alignment, stable support, and fresh target information. Execution combines the kicking teacher with a residual correction. It must then return to a stable standing or walking state; touching the ball alone does not finish the task.

A residual adds a constrained correction to an existing action. This reduces what must be learned from scratch but inherits the teacher’s operating range. Starting a local kick far from the ball does not make it search or approach. Navigation creates a valid handoff state; the skill performs its local action.

Check your understanding

The robot is beside the ball, but its remembered position has expired. Should the kick start immediately?

Show explanation

No. Observe again and confirm the entry conditions. Proximity alone does not guarantee valid inputs.

Executing a skill is not completing a task

These policies already use joint and IMU feedback for balance. They do not yet decide where the football is, which route to take or when to kick. The next chapter designs that external-perception and planning chain.
