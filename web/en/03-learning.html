3.1 Reinforcement learning and PPO#

You have seen how a policy reads observations and outputs actions. How does it learn those actions? This section introduces rewards and returns, the Actor-Critic roles, advantage estimation and how PPO controls the size of each update.

Section 3.1 · Theory · Estimated learning time: 15 minutes

Learning from trial and error#

Reinforcement learning studies how an agent that acts repeatedly in an environment can learn better behavior from the feedback it receives. It needs no correct answer for “which action in which state”, only a signal that rates how good the behavior was.

Goals, rewards and returns#

The goal is what we ultimately want the agent to achieve. The reward is the immediate feedback at each step, our way of turning the goal into numbers. Good actions often pay off only later, so learning looks at the return: all rewards from a given step onward, each discounted.

Discounted return
\[\htmlData{sym=G_t}{G_t}=\sum_{\htmlData{sym=k_idx}{k}=0}^{\infty}\htmlData{sym=gamma}{\gamma}^{\htmlData{sym=k_idx}{k}}\,\htmlData{sym=r_t}{r_{\htmlData{sym=t_idx}{t}+\htmlData{sym=k_idx}{k}}}\]
Variable meanings
\(G_t\)
Return: the sum of all rewards from step \(t\) onward, each discounted.
\(k\)
Number of steps counted forward from the current step.
\(\gamma\)
Discount factor between 0 and 1: the closer to 1, the more distant rewards count.
\(r_t\)
Immediate reward at step \(t\).
\(t\)
Time-step index.

The closer the discount factor \(\htmlData{sym=gamma}{\gamma}\) is to 1, the more distant results count. Training adjusts the policy so that the expected return is as large as possible.

A reward is only a training signal, not task success. If the reward is poorly written, the agent may find a way to score high without completing the task, so trained results are checked against a separate success criterion.

Actor-Critic: during training, one acts and one evaluates#

Adjusting the policy directly from returns is very noisy: the same action sometimes earns a high return because of later luck, and sometimes the opposite. Actor-Critic is a training paradigm that splits the work between two networks during training:

Network

Output

Role

Actor (policy)

Action

Decides what to do; this is what is deployed after training

Critic (value function)

One number: the expected return

Judges “how good is the current situation”; used only during training

With the Critic's expectation, we can tell whether an action did better than expected. That difference is the advantage. With a positive advantage the Actor makes the action more likely; with a negative one, less likely. The Critic is trained too: its prediction should match the return actually obtained. After training only the Actor is kept; the Critic's job is done.

PPO: change a little at a time#

If one update on a batch changes the policy too much, the policy can suddenly get worse, and the next batch is then collected by that worse policy, so training easily collapses. PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) compares how likely the new and old policies are to choose the same action. Once the change passes the clipping threshold \(\htmlData{sym=epsilon}{\epsilon}\), pushing further in that direction brings no extra benefit, so each update naturally stays small.

Switch the advantage between +1 and −1 and move the ratio slider to see on which side the plateau appears. Clipping acts only on the objective and does not hard-limit the actual probability ratio; PPO makes updates steadier but does not guarantee that every update improves behavior. It is simple and suits data collected in massive parallel simulation, which is why it is widely used for robot locomotion.

Extention: the formulas for advantage estimation and the PPO objective

The Critic's temporal-difference error \(\htmlData{sym=delta_t}{\delta_t}\) compares “this step's reward plus the expectation of the next state” with “the original expectation of this state”. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) sums multi-step errors decayed by \(\htmlData{sym=gamma}{\gamma}\htmlData{sym=lambda}{\lambda}\) into a smoother advantage; at termination \(\htmlData{sym=m_t}{m_t}=0\), and expectations after termination no longer count.

Temporal-difference error and generalized advantage estimation
\[\htmlData{sym=delta_t}{\delta_t}=\htmlData{sym=r_t}{r_t}+\htmlData{sym=gamma}{\gamma}\htmlData{sym=m_t}{m_t}\htmlData{sym=V}{V}(\htmlData{sym=s_next}{s_{t+1}})-\htmlData{sym=V}{V}(\htmlData{sym=s_t}{s_t}),\qquad \htmlData{sym=A_hat}{\hat A_t}=\htmlData{sym=delta_t}{\delta_t}+\htmlData{sym=gamma}{\gamma}\htmlData{sym=lambda}{\lambda}\htmlData{sym=m_t}{m_t}\htmlData{sym=A_next}{\hat A_{t+1}}\]
Variable meanings
\(\delta_t\)
Temporal-difference error: how much better this step turned out than the Critic expected.
\(r_t\)
Immediate reward at step \(t\).
\(\gamma\)
Discount factor between 0 and 1: the closer to 1, the more distant rewards count.
\(m_t\)
Continuation flag: 0 if the episode terminates at this step, otherwise 1.
\(V\)
Value function (Critic): the return expected from a state when the current policy continues.
\(s_{t+1}\)
State at the next step.
\(s_t\)
State at the current step.
\(\hat A_t\)
Advantage estimate: how much better the action actually taken was than the Critic expected.
\(\lambda\)
GAE parameter: a compromise between looking one step ahead and many steps ahead.
\(\hat A_{t+1}\)
Advantage estimate of the next step; the recursion accumulates from the end backward.

PPO multiplies the probability ratio \(\htmlData{sym=rho}{\rho_t}\) by the advantage and takes the smaller of the unclipped and clipped terms:

PPO clipped surrogate objective
\[\htmlData{sym=rho}{\rho_t}(\htmlData{sym=theta}{\theta})=\frac{\htmlData{sym=pi_theta}{\pi_\theta}(\htmlData{sym=a_t}{a_t}\mid \htmlData{sym=o_t}{o_t})}{\htmlData{sym=pi_old}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(\htmlData{sym=a_t}{a_t}\mid \htmlData{sym=o_t}{o_t})},\qquad \htmlData{sym=L_clip}{L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}}=\htmlData{sym=E_t}{\mathbb E_t}\!\left[\htmlData{sym=min}{\min}\!\left(\htmlData{sym=rho}{\rho_t}\htmlData{sym=A_hat}{\hat A_t},\htmlData{sym=clip}{\operatorname{clip}}(\htmlData{sym=rho}{\rho_t},1-\htmlData{sym=epsilon}{\epsilon},1+\htmlData{sym=epsilon}{\epsilon})\htmlData{sym=A_hat}{\hat A_t}\right)\right]\]
Variable meanings
\(\rho_t\)
Probability ratio: the probability density of the same sampled action under the new and the old policy.
\(\theta\)
Policy network parameters (weights), updated during training.
\(\pi_\theta\)
New policy (Actor): a probability distribution over actions given the observation.
\(a_t\)
Action actually sampled at the current step.
\(o_t\)
Observation at the current step.
\(\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}\)
Old policy: the policy that sampled this batch; it stays fixed during the update.
\(L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}\)
PPO clipped surrogate objective: training increases it.
\(\mathbb E_t\)
Average over the time steps of this batch.
\(\min\)
The smaller of the two terms, keeping the more conservative estimate.
\(\hat A_t\)
Advantage estimate: how much better the action actually taken was than the Critic expected.
\(\operatorname{clip}\)
Clips the probability ratio to \(1-\epsilon\) … \(1+\epsilon\) before computing the objective.
\(\epsilon\)
Clipping threshold, commonly 0.2.

Check your understanding

Does a deployed policy still need the Critic?

Show explanation

No. The Critic is used only during training to estimate expected return and compute advantages; at deployment the Actor maps observations to actions.

Check your understanding

Why can't the run with the highest training return serve as the test result?

Show explanation

Return only measures how much reward the policy collected, and reward is not task success; that run has also already been part of training or selection. A test fixes its scenarios and success criterion in advance and counts every result.
