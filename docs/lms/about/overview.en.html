Use NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab to teach the open-source Microduck biped new skills in simulation. Train walking and slope traversal with PPO and reward design, then explore a hand-designed shooting workflow. Finally, post-train NVIDIA Isaac GR00T on demonstrations and evaluate its walking shot in simulation.

Free course · about 6 hours of study (excluding full training waits; you can stop training at any time, and validation uses checkpoints provided by the course by default).

  • NVIDIA Isaac Sim: scene, robot and RGB/ToF sensor simulation.
  • NVIDIA Isaac Lab and Newton: parallel reinforcement learning, physics simulation and policy evaluation.
  • NVIDIA Isaac GR00T and LeRobot: demonstrations, vision-language-action models and action-head post-training.
  • PPO, RSL-RL, PyTorch and TensorBoard: policy optimization, training and learning-curve analysis.
  • ROS 2 and Nav2: path planning for the onboard policy.
  • Python, Docker and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit: course code and GPU containers.
  • Basic Python experience and familiarity with the Ubuntu command line.
  • Ubuntu and an NVIDIA GPU for the labs. See "1.2 Set up the environment and get the code" for supported hardware.

After completing this course, you will be able to:

  • Run Microduck simulations in Isaac Sim and inspect a policy's observations, actions and control cycle.
  • Explain the basics of PPO and run training and validation of a walking policy with Isaac Lab and Newton.
  • Design rewards and domain randomization, and judge training results from TensorBoard curves and separate task evaluations.
  • Explain how an onboard policy uses RGB detection, ToF ranging and hand-designed stages to take a shot.
  • Use the provided demonstrations to try GR00T action-head post-training, and evaluate the provided checkpoint in simulation.
  • Explain the generalization challenges a model faces in new environments, and how environment randomization can help it adapt to those changes.
